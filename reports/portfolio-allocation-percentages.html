Why these two drafts?

Draft A — larger global foundation: 60% VWCE, 37% selected individual stocks, 3% VETH. This is my preferred direction for further review because it puts most long-term capital in broad equities while preserving room for company research. It remains a growth portfolio with 97% equities and 3% crypto; it is not a low-volatility allocation.

Draft B — more company selection: 40% VWCE, 57% individual stocks, 3% VETH. To isolate the effect of relying more on company choices, the stock portion keeps the same relative company weights as A. This has more concentration and monitoring demands. It is a comparison, not an assertion that those larger weights are suitable.

Weights are authored judgments, not an optimized return forecast. A deliberately limits the largest direct position to 6%, and the eight companies in the higher-uncertainty group to 7% combined, plus 3% Ethereum. These are proposed loss-budget choices for your peace-of-mind goal: isolated total failures should have a smaller effect. They are not universal caps, do not include all fund look-through, and do not cap total losses.

04

Patient investing, everyday security

Your priority is peace of mind, with most withdrawals far in the future. Your reserve covers about six months of essential spending, already assuming a reduced lifestyle. You regard six to twelve months as a reasonable period to find another job. You have limited financial commitments and can reduce spending. A 30% decline lasting five years would be stressful but manageable, especially if you could keep contributing. Everyday spending is in RON. You would consider partial sales. The nuclear-fuel holding is Centrus Energy (LEU).

Interpretation: patient and willing to accept substantial fluctuations, conditional on preserving everyday financial security. Financial capacity is partly supported by the reserve and spending flexibility; willingness to endure losses should not be confused with guaranteed recovery. Continued contributions are not assumed in the stress comparison. This is a descriptive, provisional profile, not a validated risk score.

Still unresolved: actual behavior during past drawdowns; whether contributions can continue during unemployment; any debt, leverage, locked holdings or near-term withdrawal beyond the reserve; reserve currency/instrument; contribution pace; broker/account type and tax residence. RON spending alone does not establish tax residence. No missing balance is treated as zero.

Reserve and investment capital

Working preference to evaluate: build protection toward twelve months of essential spending, given your job-market concern. That would add six months to the existing reserve. The trade-off is less money exposed to market growth. Retaining six months remains the comparison baseline; twelve is a proposed planning choice, not an established requirement.

Keep the reserve accessible; choose actual bank or short-maturity instruments only after checking withdrawal terms, currency and applicable protection. A stock is not an emergency reserve. FINRA emergency planning↗.

All percentages below refer only to the capital available for long-term investment after the chosen reserve is set aside. The current column describes your eighteen listed investments and excludes the existing reserve and unreported cash. If sales fund a larger reserve, the amount left to invest falls; the target percentages then apply to that smaller amount. The reserve is measured in months of essential spending. Its percentage of total wealth has not been calculated.

Why the spending currency matters

The relevant result is purchasing power in the currency of your bills. Hypothetical example: if an investment is unchanged in USD and the dollar falls 10% against RON, the investment loses 10% of its RON value. Currency movements can also help returns.

A 30% USD investment decline combined with a 10% decline in the dollar against RON produces a combined −37% return in RON. Holding a global fund through a EUR trading line does not by itself hedge its assets into RON. Trading currency and underlying economic currency exposure are different.

For the reserve, the planning objective is dependable access to essential RON spending. For the long-term portfolio, global diversification can remain appropriate. The spending currency is not a reason to concentrate in Romanian companies. Currency risk: SEC↗.

05

From a draft to an investment policy

  1. Choose whether the reserve stays at six months or moves toward twelve; any required reserve top-up has priority over additional risk-taking.
  2. Complete the missing valuation work and confirm the stress level you would accept even with contributions paused. Refresh quotes and ETF/ETN listings before implementation.
  3. Compare directing future contributions to underweight holdings with partial sales. New money avoids some realization costs but can leave concentration in place for longer. With no contribution rate supplied, the time needed to rebalance through contributions is unknown.
  4. For sales, compare broker costs, currency conversion and actual tax treatment before finalizing a rebalance. Tax residence and account type remain unknown. No after-tax trade plan is claimed and purchase averages are not used.
  5. Review after a material employment change, a reserve withdrawal, new company results, financing or thesis failure. A lower share price alone is not a reason to add money. Review weights quarterly and when a major position drifts materially; finalize numerical bands only after choosing a policy.
