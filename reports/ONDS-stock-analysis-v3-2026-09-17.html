Ondas builds and buys businesses that help customers observe, defend and operate with autonomous machines. It earns money from equipment, services and development contracts. The opportunity is real, but the combined company is still being assembled.

The investment depends on useful technology becoming repeat orders, reliable deliveries and collected cash. Buying more businesses does not by itself prove a lasting competitive advantage.

A moat is a reason customers keep choosing a supplier despite credible alternatives.

Is growth creating more value?

Sales have risen dramatically, but acquisitions explain much of the reported jump. Management also reports strong growth on a pro forma basis that treats acquired businesses as if already owned. That is useful evidence, with a different definition from reported growth.

Company-calculated pro forma growth; not the audited reported growth rate.

Why it matters

Watch sales per share and cash collections alongside headline revenue. Otherwise a bigger business can leave each shareholder with a smaller slice of uncertain economics.

Acquisitions change the scope. Pro forma organic growth is company-defined; guidance is a forecast, not revenue already earned.

Explain this

Organic growth asks how the same businesses performed. Per-share growth asks what happened to each investor’s slice.

Hypothetical example: Hypothetical: sales double while shares double; revenue per share stays unchanged.

Common trap: Combining acquired revenue, backlog and contract ceilings can count the same opportunity several times.

Detailed analysis

Reported evidenceQ2 sales of $83.772m compare with $6.273m a year earlier and $50.122m in Q1. The filing attributes about $70.0m of current-quarter revenue to businesses acquired since June 2025. This is not an organic-growth calculation; management’s separate pro forma organic figure is 85%.

InterpretationRevenue per average basic share rose much less than total revenue because the average share count expanded sharply. This still shows improvement, but revenue per share is not earnings per share and does not pay shareholders until costs and cash needs are covered.

InterpretationManagement guides Q3 revenue to $140–155m and FY2026 to $525–550m. With only $133.894m recognized in H1, the annual range requires a much larger second half. Program timing and customer acceptance are critical; selected call Q&A did not provide precise quarterly LUS timing.

InterpretationReported June backlog was $613m; $757m includes DZYNE and Cyberhawk pro forma. Those are different perimeters. A program ceiling is not a funded order, and an order is not recognized revenue. Future reports should bridge opening backlog, new orders, acquisitions, deliveries and cancellations.

InterpretationDo not add all of GATE’s standalone 2026 revenue forecast to Ondas guidance: the acquisition closed in September and the announcement did not provide a new comparable full-year consolidated forecast. Its multi-year cumulative profit targets are also not one-year group profit.

InterpretationAudited revenue rose from $7.193m in 2024 to $50.731m in 2025, while basic EPS remained negative at −$0.61 and −$0.62. Comparable five/ten-year growth and eight-quarter histories remain incomplete. No analyst consensus is substituted for management guidance.

Does growth turn into cash?

Not yet. Ondas reports positive gross profit, but operating costs are much larger. It also consumed cash in the first half. Warrants are rights to buy shares. Changes in their accounting value can create reported profit without cash from customers.

H1 operating cash flow minus PP&E purchases; acquisitions are separate.

Stock compensation / revenue82.48%Q2 2026 · GAAP

Why it matters

The crucial improvement would be customers paying while costs grow more slowly than sales. Equity financing can support the transition, but it does not prove the business can fund itself.

Adjusted EBITDA excludes costs and is not cash flow. No normalized positive ROIC or self-funded expansion has been established.

Explain this

Profit, operating cash and free cash flow answer different questions.

Hypothetical example: Hypothetical: a $10m sale on credit raises revenue today but does not supply cash until the customer pays.

Common trap: Adding back stock compensation can make a margin look better while ownership is diluted.

Detailed analysis

Reported evidenceQ2 GAAP gross profit was $36.131m and operating loss $162.946m. Adjusted EBITDA was also negative at $50.629m, despite excluding substantial costs. Total Q2 stock compensation was $69.094m; the smaller operating-expense-only figure in the release has a different scope.

InterpretationH1 consolidated net income of $271.555m includes a $404.798m warrant revaluation gain. A decline in the accounting value of warrants can create a gain even while operations lose money. This makes a simple trailing P/E misleading as a measure of recurring earning power.

InterpretationH1 CFO was −$137.379m versus −$15.063m a year earlier. Deducting $9.093m PP&E purchases gives defined FCF of −$146.472m, versus −$15.330m. The cash drain is not just factory investment: operating losses and $89.2m working-capital absorption matter. Receivables and inventory need collection and delivery evidence.

InterpretationPP&E spending is modest relative to sales, but that does not make the whole expansion capital-light. H1 acquisition cash of $45.233m is separate; later acquisitions cost much more. The $1.898m all-other investing line was not fully decomposed, so the displayed FCF uses an explicitly narrow PP&E definition. CAPEX/CFO is not economically useful with negative CFO.

InterpretationManagement is investing in new capabilities and expects sequential improvement in Q3 adjusted EBITDA. The operating-platform profitability target is Q4 2026; the companywide target is Q4 2027. Neither is a promise of GAAP net profit or positive FCF. Sustainable ROIC above 15% remains unproven.

Valoarea afacerii evaluează operațiunile; valoarea capitalului este ce rămâne acționarilor după reconcilierea datoriei nete.
