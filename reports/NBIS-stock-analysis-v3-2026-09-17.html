What would I own?

Nebius rents the powerful computers and software that companies need to build and run AI. It must buy or secure the equipment, electricity and facilities before customers can use them. You own a growing infrastructure operator, with some smaller businesses and investments alongside it.

Why it matters

The advantage must show up in reliable service, customer retention and profit after equipment costs. Access to expensive chips alone is not a lasting competitive advantage.

Competitive advantage is promising, but durable returns and independently verified leadership are not established. Geographic revenue detail is from FY2025.

Explain this

A moat is a reason customers stay even when competitors have money and similar hardware.

Hypothetical example: Hypothetical: a cheaper server saves $10,000, but moving a production system costs $100,000. Integration can protect the incumbent.

Common trap: A temporary shortage can raise prices without creating a lasting moat.

InterpretationAudited 2025 customer revenue was $340.1m US, $137.5m UK and $52.2m elsewhere. The interim notes provide asset geography, which must not be presented as customer-revenue geography. US and European expansion creates power, permitting, export-control and geopolitical exposure.

InterpretationPotential differentiation lies in AI-specific engineering and a unified platform. Microsoft/Meta contracts validate the ability to win major customers, but those customers have bargaining power and alternatives. Software, integration and service execution must make the capacity more useful than a competing GPU rental.

CoreWeave is the listed financial comparator; Lambda and Crusoe offer product comparisons with limited equivalent public financial disclosure. AWS, Azure, Google Cloud and Oracle broaden the competitive field but are diversified businesses.

Do sales leave useful cash?

Not after today’s infrastructure spending. Customers pay substantial amounts in advance, which helps fund construction but also commits Nebius to future service. The business still needs outside funding, and positive adjusted operating figures do not equal cash available to shareholders.

First-half operating cash of $4.50bn less $8.13bn of cash equipment and intangible purchases. Acquisitions and debt repayments can require additional cash.

Deferred-revenue inflow / operating cash flow97.58%H1 2026 · GAAP

The deferred-revenue cash-flow increase was almost as large as operating cash inflow. These are customer advances and timing effects, not earned profit.

Operating margin-30.21%Q2 2026 · GAAP

After recognized operating costs, the group lost about 30 cents for each dollar of Q2 sales.

Why it matters

The central test is whether capacity eventually produces cash after realistic replacement costs. Today’s shortfall mixes expansion spending with operating losses; it is not yet proven to be only a temporary investment phase.

Customer advances improve funding, but the associated services still have to be delivered. Equipment lives, cash capital spending and operating cash must be read together.

Explain this

Free cash flow tests what remains after the defined operating and capital cash outlays. Profit, adjusted profit and cash measure different things.

Hypothetical example: Hypothetical: a customer pays $100 upfront, the service costs $50 over time, and equipment costs $120 now. Receiving $100 is neither $100 profit nor enough to finance the project.

Common trap: Adding depreciation back does not make the equipment free.

Detailed analysis

InterpretationStandardized free cash flow is continuing operating cash less cash purchases of property, equipment and intangibles; it is not a GAAP subtotal. It was −$3.63bn in H1 and −$5.88bn over the trailing year. Capital spending was 828.5% of H1 revenue and 180.5% of operating cash.

InterpretationSubtracting the $4.395bn deferred-revenue inflow from H1 operating cash leaves $109.1m before capital spending. This is a diagnostic bridge, not normalized cash flow: receivables, taxes and other working-capital movements still matter. Customer advances may themselves have financing costs.

InterpretationQ2 group adjusted EBITDA was +$236.2m, while adjusted net income was −$33.2m and reported continuing net income was −$190.4m. Adjusted EBITDA excludes depreciation, financing and other costs, including share compensation; it does not pay for replacing GPUs.

InterpretationThe apparent 77.1% margin after reported cost of revenue excludes separately presented depreciation. It is not comparable to a fully burdened software gross margin. Extending server/network life from four to five years reduced Q2 depreciation by $43m; profitability still depends on real equipment economics.

InterpretationH1 accounting profit included a $780.6m investment revaluation gain, larger than the $430.8m continuing net profit. This is not customer cash or evidence that the AI cloud already covers all costs. The audited annual figures take precedence over the earlier release.

What could go wrong?

Nebius must build quickly, keep equipment useful and finance the gap. Delays can postpone revenue while bills continue. New debt and new shares fund the opportunity, but can leave less of the eventual value for each existing share.

Why it matters

Conditional modelA large cash balance is only reassuring relative to the spending and obligations it must cover. Track the full financing bridge and share count, not just the next contract headline.

Exact current cash and fully diluted shares remain unreconciled. The annual report disclosed weaknesses in equipment accounting and TripleTen revenue controls. A June net-debt figure is not a September balance sheet.

Explain this

Dilution means the company is divided into more ownership units. New capital can still create value if it produces enough additional profit and cash.

Hypothetical example: Hypothetical: a business worth $100 split into 10 shares is $10 a share. A business worth $150 split into 20 shares is only $7.50 a share.

Common trap: Low cash interest does not make convertible debt free: repayment value and potential shares also matter.

Detailed analysis

InterpretationAt June 30, cash was $8.04bn against debt carrying value of $8.55bn: $0.50bn net debt before operating leases. July then added a $775m secured facility, and August $5.75bn of convertible principal plus exchanges of older debt for shares. Current cash cannot be obtained simply by adding proceeds to June cash: spending continued.

The newer partner-owned model could reduce Nebius capital needs, but signed commitments and economics must be checked rather than treating all expansion as asset-light.

Adding those gives 292.92m June economic share equivalents, before later issuance. The August exchanges add about 15.8m shares if completed as described. A market-data share count is not a complete dilution model.

InterpretationDebt coupons understate the full financing burden when redemption principal grows or conversion transfers value. InterpretationThe original EBIT/interest >10× test is not met: operating profit is negative. The reported interest line is net of capitalized interest and includes financing components of customer advances, so it is not the same as coupon cash paid. Net debt/FCF <4× is not meaningful with negative FCF. ROIC >15% is not demonstrated.

InterpretationVolozh has built a major technology organization, but this AI business still has a short execution record at the required scale. InterpretationKey failure conditions: repeated delivery delays, falling realized prices or utilization, customer cancellations, or funding that becomes too expensive. Investment gains cannot offset these operating problems. No open-market insider-buying signal was verified.
