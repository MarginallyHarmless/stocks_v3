Business software

Why it matters: Broadcom has both an AI growth engine and an established software business. It still depends heavily on a few big customers.

Does growing revenue leave useful cash?

Sales are useful only if the business can turn them into money it can use. Here we look at cash generated after running the business and buying equipment. This measure is called free cash flow.

About $46of free cash flow for every $100 of salesLatest quarter · a way of expressing the 46.2% cash-flow margin

Why it matters: This is a strength: it gives Broadcom money to pay dividends, reduce debt or invest. It does not mean all of that cash is available to shareholders.

Important limit: this calculation does not deduct acquisitions or every financing commitment. Paying employees in shares also has a cost to shareholders, even when it does not use cash immediately.

How can profit and cash be different?

Accounting profit and cash movement are recorded differently. A company can make a sale before it collects the money.

Hypothetical example. You sell a service for $100 today, but the customer pays next month. Today’s profit can rise while today’s bank balance does not.

Keep in mind. Strong cash flow is useful evidence, but timing and the chosen definition still matter. It is not the same as the cash already in the bank.

04

What could go wrong?

A few customers matter a lot

The five biggest end-customers account for 55% of quarterly sales. One customer delaying orders can have a large effect.

Large promises to buy in the future

Broadcom has legally binding promises to make $126.8 billion of future purchases. These promises could become a burden if customers buy less than expected.

Helping cover a customer’s equipment payments

Broadcom has agreed to back certain payments for a customer’s leased AI equipment. If the customer cannot pay, Broadcom could have to help. The largest potential amount at risk is $29 billion once all the equipment is installed.

No payment under this agreement was reported. The maximum is an amount that could be at risk under specific conditions. Any actual loss would depend on the contract and how much money could be recovered from the equipment.

Why it matters: Ask whether customers will keep ordering and paying, and whether Broadcom can meet its promises without damaging the value of each share.

Other risks include reliance on a major chip manufacturer, software pricing or renewal resistance, and trade restrictions.

Is the share price reasonable?

A strong business can still be an expensive investment. At the research date, one Broadcom share cost $339.51. To judge that price, compare it with the profit associated with one share.

The price-to-earnings ratio, or P/E, tells you how many dollars you pay for each $1 of annual profit. The profit definition and the year you use can change the answer dramatically.

Start with published accounting profit43.4×

Using the last 12 months of profit under standard accounting rules.

Then exclude some expenses34.8×

Using the last 12 months of “adjusted” profit. This leaves out some expenses, including paying employees in shares and certain costs linked to acquisitions.

Then assume future profit growth ESTIMATE17.5×

Using analysts’ expected adjusted profit for fiscal 2027. That profit has not been earned yet.

The share price is the same in all three rows. The lower ratio comes from counting profit differently and expecting much more of it.

Why it matters: Broadcom looks much more affordable if the expected earnings arrive. If they do not—or investors pay less for each dollar of earnings—the share price can fall even while the company keeps growing.

Does a P/E of 20 mean I get my money back in 20 years?

No. P/E is a comparison between price and annual earnings. Earnings can change, and they are not all paid directly to shareholders.

Hypothetical example. A share costs $100 and has $5 of annual earnings: P/E is 20. If earnings fall to $4 and the price stays $100, P/E becomes 25.

Keep in mind. A low ratio is not automatically a bargain. It may reflect weak prospects or risks.

Why do companies publish “adjusted” profit?

They remove certain expenses to show a particular view of operating performance. Here, stock compensation and amortization of acquired assets are among the exclusions. Amortization spreads an acquisition-related asset cost over accounting periods.

Hypothetical example. If a company reports $10 of profit and excludes $2 of costs, adjusted profit becomes $12. The two figures answer different accounting questions.

Keep in mind. An expense being excluded does not make it economically irrelevant.

Un P/E de 20 înseamnă că îmi recuperez banii în 20 de ani?

Nu. P/E compară prețul cu profitul anual. Profitul se poate schimba și nu este distribuit integral acționarilor.

Exemplu ipotetic. O acțiune costă 100 USD și are profit anual de 5 USD: P/E este 20. Dacă profitul scade la 4 USD, iar prețul rămâne 100 USD, P/E devine 25.

De reținut. Un raport mic nu înseamnă automat o oportunitate. Poate reflecta perspective slabe sau riscuri.

De ce publică firmele profit „ajustat”?

Elimină anumite cheltuieli pentru a prezenta o perspectivă asupra performanței operaționale. Aici sunt excluse, printre altele, plata în acțiuni și amortizarea activelor achiziționate. Amortizarea repartizează costul unui activ pe perioade contabile.

Exemplu ipotetic. Dacă o companie raportează profit de 10 USD și exclude costuri de 2 USD, profitul ajustat devine 12 USD. Cifrele răspund unor întrebări contabile diferite.

De reținut. Faptul că o cheltuială este exclusă nu o face lipsită de importanță economică.
