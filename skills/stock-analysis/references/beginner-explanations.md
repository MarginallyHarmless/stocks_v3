# Beginner explanations

Keep the older skill's teaching sequence: a short concept, the company's actual evidence, and what it means. The report must be understandable from the guide alone; the visible “Learn the concept” card then teaches a definition, a clearly hypothetical example and one common trap. Do not repeat a full lesson in several sections; link to its main home.

The following corrected lessons preserve the original approachable intent. Adapt them to the company's context. Provide EN/RO versions when the report supports both; keep the English finance term alongside a natural Romanian explanation when useful.

| Concept | Plain-language explanation | Worked example and trap |
|---|---|---|
| Moat / avantaj competitiv durabil | A specific reason a well-funded competitor cannot easily take customers or economics. A useful clue is persistence across competitive responses. | Switching a production system can cost more than the cheaper replacement saves. High margins alone do not prove the switching cost exists. |
| Free cash flow / flux de numerar liber | Here, operating cash flow less explicitly defined cash capital spending. It tests cash generation after those investments. | CFO 20 less CAPEX 7 gives FCF 13. Debt repayments, acquisitions, leases and other commitments can still limit distributable cash. FCF is not simply the bank balance. |
| Profit versus cash | Accrual profit recognizes economic activity under accounting rules; cash flow tracks cash movement with its own classifications and timing. Read both. | A credit sale can raise profit before collection. Working-capital timing can lift cash without better underlying profitability. Neither number automatically “tells the truth” alone. |
| Net debt / datorie netă | Defined interest-bearing debt minus eligible accessible cash; disclose leases and restricted cash treatment. | Debt 50 minus usable cash 60 is net cash 10. Gross maturities can still matter even with net cash. |
| EBITDA | Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization. Useful in some operating comparisons, with limitations. | Depreciation is added back although assets eventually need replacement. EBITDA omits working-capital needs and CAPEX; it is not cash available for debt repayment. |
| Dilution / diluție | More shares can reduce a fixed holding's percentage ownership. Check whether additional capital also grows value per share. | A 10% share increase reduces an unchanged ownership percentage by 1−1/1.10 = 9.09%, not 10%. Weighted-average diluted shares are an EPS denominator, not exact point-in-time ownership. |
| Buybacks / răscumpărări | Company purchases can reduce net shares or offset issuance. Effectiveness depends on price paid, alternative uses and financing. | Buying 5 shares while issuing 5 leaves the count unchanged, but offsets dilution that otherwise would occur. Flat shares do not mean the spending had literally no effect. An all-time-high price is not proof of overvaluation. |
| SBC / plata în acțiuni | Equity compensation is a real economic cost even when noncash in the current cash-flow statement. | SBC is generally added back under indirect CFO, but share issuance or repurchases can affect holders. Do not claim SBC has no cash consequences under all arrangements. |
| P/E | Price divided by compatible positive earnings per share. It describes the price of a unit of earnings. | Price 50 / EPS 2 = 25×. That does not promise payback in 25 years: earnings change and are not all distributed. |
| P/FCF | Equity price relative to consistently defined cash flow attributable to shareholders. | Different CAPEX and working-capital definitions can change it. A gap from P/E is a question to investigate, not proof one number is correct. |
| Earnings yield | EPS / price, the inverse of compatible positive P/E. | P/E 25 implies 4%. This is not a guaranteed dividend or bond coupon; growth, risk and accounting differ. |
| EV/EBITDA | Enterprise value divided by enterprise operating earnings before depreciation/amortization. It partly separates operating comparison from financing. | EV needs a disclosed debt/cash/preferred/minority-interest bridge. EBITDA can omit major real capital needs. It is not a universal valuation improvement. |
| ROIC | Defined after-tax operating earnings divided by average invested capital. It asks what the business earns on capital deployed. | NOPAT 15 / average capital 100 = 15%. Accounting, acquisitions and capital definition affect comparability. A high figure alone does not establish a moat. |
| Gross margin / marjă brută | Revenue remaining after reported cost of revenue. Changes can reflect price, volume/mix, input cost or classification. | Revenue 100 less cost 60 gives 40%. A fall need not mean lost pricing power; test the cause. |
| Beta | A historical regression estimate of stock sensitivity to a chosen market over a chosen period. | Beta 2 with a hypothetical −10% market move gives −20% in a simplified beta-only model; actual results can differ greatly. It is not a guaranteed loss amount, full risk measure or forecast. Show actual drawdowns separately. |
| Risk-free rate | A model input using a suitable sovereign yield, currency and horizon. | A nominal bond held to maturity has different interest-rate/inflation/currency risks from a stock. Do not equate every government bond with no risk in every context. |
| Multiple compression | Price can fall if the multiple investors pay declines, even as earnings rise. | EPS grows from 2 to 3 while P/E falls from 30 to 15: modeled price falls from 60 to 45. This demonstrates a mechanism, not the actual cause of a particular price move. |
| Benchmark | A relevant alternative, measured over matching dates, currency and dividend conventions. | Stock +12% and index +18% means 6 percentage points of relative underperformance. It does not by itself prove the original decision was wrong. |
| Total shareholder return | Price change plus dividends, with reinvestment convention specified. | A stock from 100 to 108 plus a 2 dividend gives a simple 10% holding-period return before costs/taxes. Do not add buyback yield again: effects can already be reflected in price. |
| Shareholder yield | Dividends and net repurchases relative to equity market value, with the definition stated. | This is a corporate payout measure, not total shareholder return. Gross repurchases can obscure share issuance. |
| Beat/miss and guidance | Compare actuals with dated compatible expectations; distinguish analyst consensus from management guidance. | A quarter can beat the previous quarter yet miss consensus. A good result and a falling stock can coexist; investigate expectations and valuation without asserting a single cause. |
| Deceleration | Growth remains positive but slows. | 60% → 40% → 30% is slower growth, not falling revenue. Its valuation significance depends on what was expected and cash/profit consequences. |
| Conditional expectations | Required business outcomes under an explicit valuation model and chosen assumptions. | Market value 1,000 / assumed P/S 5 gives revenue 200. At P/S 4 it gives 250. The chosen multiple determines the requirement; neither is a uniquely revealed market forecast. |
| Cash runway | A conditional estimate of how long available funding covers assumed cash needs. | Cash 40 / burn 10 per quarter = four quarters under constant burn and funding assumptions. It is not a guaranteed date, and missing evidence does not prove structural weakness. |
| Short interest / float | Open short positions relative to a defined available share base on an observation date. | Daily short-sale volume is a different quantity. A large percentage can reflect hedging or other strategies, not just a uniform bearish conviction. |
| Insider and institutional activity | Dated disclosures of specific transactions/positions, with varying scope and lag. | Separate open-market purchases, awards, options, tax withholding and prearranged plans. Insider buying is evidence, not proof of a single motive or future return. |
| Dual-class shares | Economic ownership and voting power can differ. | A founder may retain control without majority economic ownership. Explain minority-holder consequences; do not presume good or bad governance solely from structure. |

## Romanian phrasing examples

**FCF:** „Fluxul de numerar liber arată numerarul generat de operațiuni după investițiile definite în calcul. În cazul acestei companii, verificăm exact ce investiții sunt scăzute. Nu înseamnă că toți banii rămași pot fi distribuiți acționarilor.”

**Diluție:** „Dacă afacerea este împărțită în mai multe acțiuni, aceeași deținere reprezintă un procent mai mic. Important este dacă banii obținuți prin emisiune cresc suficient valoarea fiecărei acțiuni.”

**Preț versus afacere:** „O afacere bună nu este automat o investiție atractivă la orice preț. Separăm ce știm despre companie de ce trebuie să se întâmple pentru a justifica prețul actual.”

**Unknown versus adverse:** „Datele publicate nu rezolvă încă întrebarea. Aceasta este o limită a informațiilor disponibile, nu dovada unui rezultat negativ.”

## Do not import the old shortcuts

Avoid deterministic beta losses, rigid moat rankings, “missing a phase condition means structural”, “cash always tells the truth”, mandatory sell signals from a low cyclical P/E, guaranteed price effects from interest rates, one-year benchmark verdicts, and a reverse multiple described as the market's exact forecast. Use each as an investigative question with evidence and limitations.
