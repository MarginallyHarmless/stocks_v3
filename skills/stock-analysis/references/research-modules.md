# Research modules

## Sources and retrieval

Start at the regulator's filing index and issuer investor-relations results/calendar pages. For US issuers, resolve CIK before EDGAR company submissions/companyfacts retrieval; inspect filing context, unit, fiscal period and amendments, not merely a matching XBRL label. If one public route fails, use the issuer's mirrored filing/release or another accessible original. Record access failures and dates. Do not hard-code providers as permanently accessible or inaccessible.

Use a market-data provider for timestamped prices/adjusted histories. Secondary aggregators such as Stock Analysis, Yahoo Finance or Morningstar can locate data and provide explicitly dated estimates; trace decisive historical financial figures back to filings. Use local regulators/exchanges for non-US issuers. Batch independent reads; sequence identity resolution, peer selection and reconciliation when they depend on prior findings. No paid connector is required.

## What is happening with the stock?

Show matched-date stock, broad-index and justified sector returns. Identify currency, split adjustment, dividends and start/end dates. Explain observed news/events separately from hypotheses about the price move. A coincident announcement does not prove causality; price can reflect several changing expectations. Sentiment belongs here, not in business quality.

Three-year return can exceed five-year return: prices 100 five years ago, 50 three years ago, 200 today yield +100% and +300%. Investigate listing changes/corporate actions separately; this arithmetic is not an anomaly.

## Is operating momentum improving?

Aim for approximately eight comparable quarters where available. Compare revenue, operating profitability and relevant company-specific metrics; distinguish YoY from QoQ and seasonality. Track organic versus acquired growth, constant-currency versus reported changes, business totals versus per-share growth, diluted weighted-average versus point-in-time shares. A shorter public history is missing history, not failure.

Use a short visible interpretation and 1–3 decisive metrics per section. Show the rest in a sourced trend/table. Do not smooth away a bad quarter or infer a trend from one observation.

## Is weak cash flow temporary, structural, mixed or unresolved?

Separate cash consumed by investment from recurring operating losses. Assess committed funding, accessible cash, realistic cash needs, maturities, funding conditions and dated operating milestones. Cash runway is a conditional burn-rate model, not a guaranteed survival date. Restricted cash and conditional facilities are not equivalent to immediately available cash.

Use four cause judgments: temporary investment pressure, structural weakness, mixed causes, unresolved cause. Missing funding or milestone evidence does not prove structural failure. Even funded investment can fail; the cause label does not neutralize the risk. Identify evidence that would distinguish the explanations in the next watchlist.

## What operating results justify the price?

Reverse multiples are conditional models. For an equity P/S anchor, required revenue = equity market value / selected P/S. Explain the anchor, horizon, future dilution and market-value assumption. For EV/sales, use an enterprise-value bridge rather than equity market value. Show sensitivity to at least two plausible anchors; do not say the market uniquely predicts that revenue.

Trailing/forward P/E ratios can imply earnings growth only with compatible price, EPS basis, share definition, currency and forecast conventions. The ratio is about those denominators; it does not uniquely reveal investor expectations.

Retain bear/base/bull scenarios when meaningful. Align valuation dates/horizons, expose assumptions, and distinguish terminal future price from discounted present value. Show which input dominates. Use unavailable outputs if inputs do not support a model. A numerical range is allowed; manufactured precision/probabilities are not.

## Sector and cross-company comparisons

Retain the inherited applicability rules. Banks: credit quality, capital, deposit funding/cost and banking returns; do not force industrial FCF or gross-margin tests. Insurers: underwriting, reserves, investment exposure and solvency. REITs: reconciled FFO/AFFO, property economics and leverage. Unprofitable infrastructure firms: funded commitments, conversion of contracts into cash, utilization, dilution and cash runway. Cyclicals: normalized through-cycle earnings and working-capital effects. Recent IPOs: public-history limits and share-class/lock-up changes.

Compare the same relevant questions, including management and capital allocation. Justify 3–5 direct peers where available; fewer are acceptable with a reason. Align financial definitions and periods or explicitly label Not comparable. A one-year benchmark shortfall alone does not establish a bad investment decision or a weak business.
